## 1. Process

1.1 Standard supplier onboarding takes **10 business days** from a complete
request. If the supplier handles cold-chain product, add a QA audit and plan for
20 business days.

1.2 The six steps are:

1. Supplier request submitted by the business owner.
2. Due diligence: legal entity, ownership, sanctions screening.
3. Risk assessment: financial, food safety, data protection.
4. Contract and insurance review.
5. Systems setup: vendor record, payment details, access if required.
6. Probation: first 90 days monitored by the business owner.

1.3 A request missing documents is returned, not queued. The clock restarts when
the request is complete.

## 2. Required documents

- Certificate of incorporation and VAT number.
- Food safety certification for any supplier handling chilled or frozen product.
- Proof of insurance with a minimum cover of **EUR 5 million**.
- Signed data processing agreement when personal data is involved.
- Bank verification letter in the supplier's legal name.

## 3. Approval thresholds

| Contract value | Approver |
| --- | --- |
| Up to EUR 50,000 | Procurement lead |
| EUR 50,001 to EUR 250,000 | Chief Financial Officer |
| Above EUR 250,000 | Board resolution |

3.1 Thresholds apply to the total contract value, not the annual value. A
three-year contract at EUR 90,000 per year is above EUR 250,000 and requires a
board resolution.

## 4. Probation

4.1 The first 90 days are probation. The business owner records a review at day
30, day 60 and day 90.

4.2 A failed probation blocks new purchase orders until Procurement and the
business owner agree on a remediation plan.

## 5. Rejection and appeals

A rejected supplier receives a written reason within 5 business days. The
supplier may appeal once, in writing, within 30 days. The appeal is reviewed by
Procurement and Legal; the decision is final.

## 6. Conflicts of interest

Any employee with a personal or financial relationship to a supplier declares it
before the request is submitted. Undeclared relationships are a disciplinary
matter, not a procurement detail.
